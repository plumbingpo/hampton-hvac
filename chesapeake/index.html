A little caution before we arrive can head off a much larger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be turned off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen risks an overflowing drain pan and a damaged compressor. A short-cycling furnace, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us immediately.

How Chesapeake Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

Take a look at the age of your system. That number says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that range and is also showing rising bills, uneven temperatures, or frequent repairs, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install when the first heat wave hits.

The earlier you report a comfort problem, the cheaper it usually is. A weak capacitor costs little to replace, yet that same capacitor, ignored for a season, can take out the compressor and force a full system replacement. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away the moment something sounds or feels wrong in your home. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
